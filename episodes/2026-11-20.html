    # When the Training Signal Lies to Itself


    A cluster of research published today challenges one of the AI industry's core assumptions: that reinforcement learning — the technique labs use to sharpen models after they're built — can produce genuinely new capabilities. A landmark paper shows it can only amplify behaviors already present in the original training data, not create new ones. That finding lands against a backdrop of broken benchmarks, biased AI judges, compromised training data, and agents being exploited through their own helpfulness — four problems that are really one.
